How it works

Your camera, now a witness.

QoreWitness seals the video from your home camera within minutes of recording. Here is what happens, step by step, in plain words.

Your video stays at home.

The clips never leave your box. The public network gets only short fingerprints, the times and a signed summary. A fingerprint cannot show a picture or a sound. The network can see the account address of your box and when each seal was made.

The letter metaphorYou press a wax seal on a letter. You keep the letter in a safe place. A postmark shows when it was sent. Anyone can look at the seal and the postmark. In QoreWitness the seal is a digital signature. The postmark is the record on the public network.

Four steps

Fingerprint, bundle, sign, record.

This runs on its own while your camera records.

  1. 01

    Fingerprint

    The box cuts the stream into clips of one minute. Each clip gets two independent digital fingerprints (SHA-256 and SHA3-256). Change one byte and the fingerprints change.

  2. 02

    Bundle

    Every 10 minutes, the box bundles the fingerprints of that window into one short summary. This summary is called a Merkle root. Each clip is also linked to the clip before it.

  3. 03

    Sign

    Your camera has its own digital key. It signs the summary with ML-DSA-87. This is the seal.

  4. 04

    Record

    The smart contract on QoreChain checks the signature. If it fits, the contract stores the summary and the time. A receipt file stays next to your clips.

The picture

What goes where.

  1. Stays at home

    Your camera

    A Tapo C210 or another camera with a network video stream.

  2. Stays at home

    Your box

    Records the clips. Makes fingerprints. Signs the summary. Keeps clips and receipts.

  3. QoreChain testnet

    Checks the signature. Stores the summary and the time. Holds no video.

  4. Any browser

    The verifier takes a clip and a receipt. It repeats every check against the public network.

Built for later

Post-quantum, in two sentences.

Common signatures such as ECDSA and RSA may break when large quantum computers exist. QoreWitness signs with ML-DSA-87, a signature standard (NIST FIPS 204) built to resist them, and the network checks every signature before it accepts a record.

Evidence can be needed many years after recording. That is why the signature type matters.

What it cannot do

  • It cannot show that the scene was real. A staged scene is sealed the same way.
  • It cannot see what happens to the video before the box seals it. Keep the box and the camera safe.
  • It is not an EU qualified time stamp. A court weighs it as ordinary evidence.
  • On the testnet pilot, records may be removed when the network resets.
