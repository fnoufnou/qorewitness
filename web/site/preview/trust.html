Trust and limits

What it proves, and what it cannot.

A proof is only as good as what goes into it. Here is the plain list. We publish what we catch and what we miss.

What it proves

  • This file existed at the time of the network record. It was sealed within minutes of recording.
  • It has not changed since it was sealed.
  • The seal was made with the key of one known camera.
  • The clip sits in an unbroken recording. A removed, inserted or re-ordered clip shows. A gap in recording shows with its start and length.
  • The time is bounded on both sides. The seal was made after a recent block and recorded before a later block.

What it cannot prove

  • That the scene was real. A staged scene or a fake stream is sealed the same way.
  • Who recorded it. It shows which camera key signed.
  • That nobody fed the box fake footage before sealing. The box is the trust boundary.
  • That a court accepts it. We are not an EU qualified time stamp provider. A court weighs it as ordinary evidence.

Threat model, summary

Who could attack it, and what happens.

We ran drills on the live system. Results are from our own tests. No outside firm ran them. Read the full texts: threat model and custody drill.

AttackResultWhy
Change one byte of a sealed clipDetectedThe file fingerprint is no longer in the receipt.
Remove or re-order a clip in the receiptDetectedThe chain of clips breaks.
Seal a second version of an old windowRefusedThe contract refuses overlapping windows.
Swap in footage from another camera, re-encode, delete or add a clip before sealingFlaggedThe record shows a stream change, a gap or an overlap. In our drill, 4 of 5 such attacks were flagged.
Swap in footage that uses the camera's own settings, before sealingMissedThe box cannot tell. This is the trust boundary.
Steal the boxRiskThe box holds your camera key and, today, the keys of your test account. Keep the box safe. A mode that keeps the main key offline is planned.
An attacker with only the classic (ECDSA) key of the accountFailedIn a dry run of the Q-Day Drill, 13 attacks failed and 0 succeeded. Every change needs a fresh post-quantum signature.

Testnet pilot: what can go wrong

  • The test network can reset. The QoreChain team says it will not reset before 15 Dec 2026. After that, a reset can remove every pilot record.
  • Pilot records are not evidence yet. They show how the method works.
  • Fees included depends on us. The pilot operator (fnoufnou) pays the fees with test tokens. The service can stop. You can then switch to Self-managed.
  • The main network is not live for this yet. The post-quantum checks run on the testnet today. When the main network opens, you would need to seal again.

Privacy

What leaves your home.

This is a summary and not legal advice. It reflects the GDPR (AVG) as we understand it.

PartDataOur rule
Your boxVideo of people at and near your home. Camera address and camera password.Video stays on the box. We never receive it. The box sends no usage data to us.
Public networkAccount address, camera id, times and fingerprints.Fixed rhythm. Fingerprints show no content. We use no names or addresses as labels.
Public network nodeThe IP address of your box can be seen by the node it talks to.Known limit.
This websiteYour IP address in server logs.No trackers. No email is collected in the pilot.
  • Records on a public network cannot be deleted. Keep names and addresses out of camera labels.
  • A fingerprint of a video with people in it may count as personal data in some cases. We keep everything else off the chain.
If your camera films the street or a neighbourThe household exemption likely does not apply then (CJEU, case C-212/13, Rynes). You are the controller of that video. Aim the camera at your own property. Put up a sign. Read the guidance on home cameras from your data protection authority. In the Netherlands this is the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens.
